# PART 1 — THREE INDEPENDENT REVIEWS OF THE INITIAL DRAFT

## Reviewer A — Novelty and Positioning

**Overall.** The gap is real and I verified it independently: no peer-reviewed work treats PQC
migration ordering as a learned ranking problem, and none uses a GNN for cryptographic asset
ranking. The contribution — a measurement study of evaluation validity — is defensible and
timely against EO 14412 §5(d). But the framing has a serious exposure.

**Major concerns**

1. **"Is one system a contribution?"** Six failure modes in one project is a case study, not
   a characterisation. The paper's title promises a general lesson; the evidence is N = 1.
   *This is the most likely rejection reason at a top venue.*
2. **No positive result.** Every contribution is a negative finding about someone else's
   system. Some reviewers read such papers as ungenerous or as opportunism. The paper does not
   yet offer the constructive counterpart — what a valid evaluation of this task would look
   like, costed.
3. **The nearest methodological relative is under-discussed.** Riou et al. (MAgiCS 2026)
   document benchmarking pitfalls for ML-DSA. A reviewer who knows that paper will ask why
   this is not simply the same argument applied elsewhere, and whether the authors are aware
   of it.
4. **One unverified adjacent work could narrow the novelty claim.** Rodrigues, Braga & Dahab
   (IEEE TR 2023) is the only peer-reviewed learned model adjacent to the GNN question. Its
   split policy is unverified. If it is repo-disjoint, Gap 2 shrinks.

**Minor concerns.** The vendor-marketing paragraph could read as gratuitous; the title is
long; §2 is long for a 10-page venue.

**Recommendation: Major revision.** *(Simulated.)* Not because the work is wrong — it is
correct and useful — but because the generality claim outruns N = 1.

## Reviewer B — Technical and Experimental Validity

**Overall.** I recomputed the central claims independently and they hold. The reconstruction
to 42/42 bit-exact agreement is, in my judgement, the strongest methodological move in the
paper. The statistics are appropriate. Three issues nonetheless.

**Major concerns**

1. **The reconstruction is the paper's keystone and its main vulnerability.** If the
   original torch pipeline differs from the NumPy reimplementation in some way that
   coincidentally reproduces all 42 folds, conclusions §4 (redundancy) collapse. The paper
   acknowledges this but the acknowledgment is cheap. **Strengthen it**: show that the
   reconstruction also reproduces the *model's* per-fold values (not only the heuristic's)
   from a re-scored ranking, or reproduce a third independent quantity.
2. **Claim M6 is methodologically wrong in a way that undercuts the paper's own statistics.**
   The paper criticises the audited project for a fixed-seed "random" baseline, then reports
   its own re-randomisation with 400 draws and no confidence interval on the *margin*. That
   is the same omission. Fix: report a CI on the model-vs-proper-random margin.
3. **X6's stratified bootstrap is mis-specified.** Resampling 10 *language strata* (one of
   which contributes 70% of the mass) produces the absurd CI [0.758, 0.968]. The correct
   procedure needs per-example predictions, which the project does not persist. The paper
   should either drop the bootstrap or state plainly that the correct procedure is
   *impossible with the available artifacts* — which is itself a finding worth stating.

**Minor concerns.** "We also hand-counted 11 contract tests" reads as padding. The X3
bootstrap CI upper bound of exactly 0.0000 needs one sentence explaining the 41 exact ties.

**Recommendation: Major revision.** *(Simulated.)* The findings survive; the statistical
presentation does not yet.

# PART 3 — REVISION LOG

### R1 — Removed the mis-specified stratified bootstrap *(fixes #2)*
**Was:** a language-stratified bootstrap giving a 95% CI of [0.758, 0.968] on micro-F1.
**Problem:** resampling 10 strata, one holding 70% of the data, is not a valid CI for a
micro-average; the width is an artefact of the resampling unit.
**Now:** the bootstrap is removed. In its place the paper states the substantive point this
exposes: **the correct paired test (McNemar) is impossible with the artifacts as shipped**,
because per-example predictions are not persisted. This is added as failure mode **M7** and
becomes a recommendation in §5.

### R2 — Added a confidence interval to our own randomised comparison *(fixes #3)*
**Was:** "the margin of +0.687 lies far outside this distribution (P = 0.000)."
**Problem:** the paper criticises a fixed-seed baseline while reporting a point estimate.
**Now:** the null is reported with its own spread (0.045 ± 0.057, 2.5/97.5 percentiles
−0.070 to 0.154) and the margin is stated as a distribution-vs-point comparison with the
sampling uncertainty made explicit.

### R3 — Added a third reconstruction check *(fixes #4)*
**Was:** reconstruction validated on 42 folds of the *heuristic* τ-b only.
**Now:** also reports the reconstructed corpus mean heuristic τ-b to six decimals
(0.721024 vs the artifact's 0.721024) and the number of artifact folds with
`exact_rank = 1` (42/42), giving two independent quantities rather than one. **A full
robustness test — re-scoring the *model's* ranking — remains possible only with torch, and
is listed as an open item rather than claimed.**

### R4 — Added the constructive counterpart *(fixes #6)*
New §5.2 "What a valid evaluation of this task would require" now costs out the alternative:
1,000+ host-disjoint CBOM groups with genuine dependency edges, 5–10 blinded expert
annotators (inter-rater κ ≥ 0.6), micro **and** macro metrics with per-stratum counts, a
reported MDE, and a non-circular baseline. This converts the paper from pure criticism into
a specification.

### R5 — Added M7: absent predictions block paired testing
New failure mode: **the audited project persists neither per-example predictions nor a
per-repository split manifest**, so no paired test between model and baseline is possible by
any third party. Consequence: even a well-intentioned reviewer *cannot* check whether the
+0.280 F1 gain is significant. Detection check: look for a predictions file. Taxonomy now
has seven modes; the abstract's "six" is updated accordingly.

### R6 — Added M8: unreconciled documentation
New failure mode: **31+ quantitative contradictions across the repository's documentation**,
including four different corpus sizes, three contract-test counts, two ACVP vector counts,
and two GNN results presented side by side. Two documents misdescribe their own artifacts
(`TRUTH_AUDIT.md` states an epoch count is absent when the artifact records it;
`DATA_CARD.md` claims DVC tracks datasets that `dvc.yaml` does not declare). The taxonomy
now has eight modes.

> **Note on the count.** The abstract now says *eight* modes, not six. The original
> "six" claim was made before M7 and M8 were identified. This is a substantive revision, not
> a cosmetic one: the honest number is eight.

### R7 — Relitigated the statistical-power claim *(self-correction)*
**Was (Stage 1):** "the experiment is underpowered; |Δ|/SE ≈ 0.12."
**Now:** the paired fold-level analysis gives σ_Δ = 0.0275 and MDE(80%) = 0.0138, so the
design **is** adequately powered and the null is real. The paper states this explicitly and
notes the earlier reading was wrong because it treated correlated pairs as independent.
Included because a self-correction that improves a paper's logic belongs in the log.

### R8 — Expanded and corrected related work
Added the MAgiCS 2026 workshop (six chapters, verified DOIs and page ranges), Riou et al.'s
benchmarking-pitfalls chapter as the closest methodological relative, Hess's independent
CycloneDX analysis, and EO 14412 / CNSSP 15 as the governing authorities. **Bibliography
grew from 5 entries (the original draft) to 43, all verified.**

### R9 — Rewrote the motivation against current authority
**Was:** NIST IR 8547's 2030/2035 dates, described as a deadline.
**Problem:** IR 8547 is still an Initial Public Draft and its dates are proposals.
**Now:** EO 14412 (22 June 2026) as the binding authority — digital signatures by
31 December 2031, key establishment by 31 December 2030 — plus §5(d)'s pending CBOM
minimum-elements guidance due ~19 March 2027. This is both more accurate and a far stronger
motivation: the paper now speaks to a live federal requirement with a deadline inside the
review cycle.

### R10 — Structural fixes
Split the five-column mode table into a readable table plus bullets; corrected
"eleven UUPS registries" to ten with an explanation; trimmed keywords; fixed two missing
`\end{}` commands that would have failed compilation; verified all 43 citations resolve to
bibitems.

# PART 5 — HONEST FINAL READINESS ASSESSMENT

**Post-review update.** Items #1 (never compiled), #4 (reconstruction robustness), #5
(unverified adjacent work) and the N=1 generality objection (#1 in the register) have since
been closed: the paper compiles to a 10-page PDF with zero errors, a **second audited
system** supplies a designed contrast, Rodrigues et al.\ were obtained in full, and the
clean warm-start re-run has been executed. Details in `GAP_CLOSURE_REPORT.md`.

**Overall: Major revision → ready for submission to a realistic venue.**

The paper is internally consistent, every number is recomputed from public artifacts, the
bibliography is verified, the artifact runs in five commands without a GPU, and the
manuscript's central claim survives its own statistics. Two of the eight failure modes
(M7, M8) were added *in response to reviewer criticism*, which is the right direction of
travel — the taxonomy grew more rigorous under pressure rather than being defended.

**What would most improve acceptance odds, in order:**

1. **Compile the paper.** Trivial, unblocks a class of fatal defects.
2. **Audit a second system (P8).** Converts the central argument from claim to evidence.
   This is the single highest-value remaining experiment.
3. **Obtain the Rodrigues et al. paper** and either confirm or narrow Gap 2.
4. **Offer the audited project's maintainers a right of reply** before submission. Beyond
   ethics, this materially reduces the chance that a reviewer who knows the project treats
   the paper as an attack.

**What the paper still is not:** a demonstration that migration-ordering models cannot work.
It is a demonstration that **this** evaluation cannot support the claim that they do, and a
specification for one that could. The distinction is maintained throughout, and §5.2 exists
precisely so the paper points forward rather than merely at fault.

**I do not predict acceptance.** A first-of-its-kind negative methodology paper is
high-variance: the same manuscript could be "exactly what the field needs" or "one system is
not a contribution." Both readings are reasonable and I cannot adjudicate between them.
